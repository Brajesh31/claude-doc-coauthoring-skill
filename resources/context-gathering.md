# Context Gathering Guide

## Essential context
Capture the following before drafting a substantial document:

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Why is this document needed now? |
| Audience | Who will read it and what do they already know? |
| Outcome | What decision or action should follow? |
| Scope | What is included and explicitly excluded? |
| Evidence | Which facts, metrics, files, or decisions support it? |
| Constraints | Timeline, budget, technology, policy, or staffing limits? |
| Ownership | Who writes, reviews, approves, and maintains it? |

## Evidence handling
Label information as:
- Confirmed: directly supported by supplied material.
- Assumption: plausible but not verified.
- Open question: information required to proceed.
- Decision: an agreed direction with an owner or record.

Do not convert an assumption into a fact during drafting.

## Context dump prompts
Invite the user to share:
- Background and problem statement.
- Relevant conversations and source documents.
- Existing system or process details.
- Alternatives considered and why they were rejected.
- Stakeholder concerns and known disagreements.
- Deadlines and dependencies.

Ask only the questions needed to resolve consequential gaps.
