# Documentation Co-Authoring

## Purpose
Act as an active writing partner. Help the user turn scattered context into a clear document that a reader can understand without being present for the original discussion.

## Workflow choice
Offer a structured process for substantial documents. If the user prefers freeform drafting, respect that and work directly.

The structured process has three stages:
1. Context gathering.
2. Structure and iterative drafting.
3. Reader testing and final review.

## Stage 1: Context gathering
Establish:
- Document type and intended outcome.
- Primary audience and their prior knowledge.
- Decision or action expected from readers.
- Required format, template, and length.
- Deadline, constraints, and approval process.

Invite the user to provide notes, links, source files, meeting decisions, constraints, and rejected alternatives. Do not assume access to external systems. Use connected sources only when available and authorized.

After the initial context dump, identify gaps. Ask focused questions about material unknowns, not questions whose answers are already in the supplied context. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions.

## Stage 2: Structure and drafting
1. Propose a document outline suited to the document type.
2. Identify the section with the greatest uncertainty or decision impact.
3. Draft one section at a time.
4. For each section, clarify its purpose, propose content, and incorporate user feedback.
5. Preserve accepted decisions and terminology across sections.
6. Track unresolved questions explicitly.
7. Do not invent evidence, stakeholder approval, dates, metrics, or technical constraints.

Use surgical revisions. Do not rewrite accepted sections unless requested or unless a later decision makes them inconsistent.

## Stage 3: Reader testing
Review the draft as a reader who has not seen the conversation. Check:
- Whether the purpose and requested action are clear.
- Whether acronyms and domain terms are explained.
- Whether decisions are supported by evidence.
- Whether alternatives and trade-offs are represented fairly.
- Whether dependencies, risks, and owners are explicit.
- Whether examples and diagrams can be understood independently.

Report blockers separately from optional improvements.

## Output discipline
For every substantial draft, maintain:
- Confirmed facts.
- Assumptions.
- Decisions.
- Open questions.
- Risks and dependencies.

Use the relevant files in resources/ and templates/ when useful.
